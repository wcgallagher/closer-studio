# Human Tradecraft

Two jobs: make the speaker read as the most credible person in the room, and make the words move a buyer forward
without tricking them. Pair every script pass with `trust-scorecard`: influence that a buyer later feels was a trick
kills retention, referrals and, in insurance, licenses.

## Rules (always on)

- No corporate or helpful-assistant padding. Short, human sentences. Fragments are fine.
- Banned: crucial, delve, testament, "in today's digital landscape", unleash, furthermore, "tap into", plus the
  house-rules list.
- If a fact, number or file is not in the workspace or transcript, it does not exist. Write
  `[DATA GAP: <what is missing>]` instead of inventing it.

## 1. Transcript audit: the 20 indicators

Score the script or transcript with `trust-scorecard` (the 20 NCI indicators). Every line at 3+ gets rewritten
before it reaches the edit.

## 2. Delivery audit (pick takes and cuts for authority)

For each candidate take, from the word-level transcript and the audio, mark:

| Signal | High authority (keep) | Low authority (cut or swap take) |
|---|---|---|
| Vocal discipline | Even pitch, statements end down | Upspeak on statements, pitch rising when nervous |
| Fillers | None inside a sentence | um, uh, like, you know, "so yeah" |
| Hedges | Direct claims | "kind of", "I think maybe", "sort of", "honestly" before a claim |
| Pace | Steady, slows down on the key number or promise | Rushing the proof, speeding up at the ask |
| Pauses | A beat before and after the main point | Dead air from losing the thread, restarts |
| Restarts | One clean delivery | Repeating the opening words, correcting mid-sentence |
| Eye line | Lens | Looking off to read or think |
| Body | Still, open hands, gestures that land on the stress word | Fidgeting, self-touch, swaying |
| Asking for approval | Statement, then silence | Tag questions ("right?", "make sense?") after every point |

Edit rule: build the cut from the takes with the most "keep" marks. Never cut a pause that sits right after the main
point; that silence is where authority lives. Tighten everything else to the next word.

## 3. Script language

Use these, and only on claims that are true:

- **Open loops:** raise a question early and pay it off later ("There's one question I ask every client that finds
  the money. I'll give it to you in a minute.").
- **Pacing and leading:** state 2 to 4 things the viewer already knows are true about their situation, then lead to
  the new idea ("You sell mortgage protection. Your clients are 50 to 70. Half of them mention a 401(k) on the call.
  That's the money you've been walking past.").
- **Specifics over adjectives:** a number, a name, a place, a date beats "huge", "amazing", "life-changing".
- **Future pacing:** let them picture the next step concretely ("Wednesday at 1 PM you're on the call, watching me
  run one live.").
- **Elicitation questions** on calls: questions that let the prospect say the reason out loud themselves instead of
  being told it.
- **One ask:** one action, said plainly, said once more at the end.

Never: fake scarcity, invented proof, fear without a fact under it, or pressure to decide before thinking. Those
score high on the trust scorecard for a reason.

## Output

1. Delivery table per take (keep / cut marks with timestamps).
2. Recommended take for each line, with the reason in one clause.
3. Rewritten script lines, each with the technique used and its trust-scorecard check.
