# Blind panel: a full-reset VSL cut against the shipped one (2026-11-02)

Two cuts of the same 16:9 homepage VSL, made from the same three phone takes: the shipped cut (94.6 s, called Q here)
and a full re-edit made with the new sound, placement, finish and hand-label engines (71.4 s, called P). Twelve blind
reviews: six lenses (direct-response editor, retention editor, cinematic designer, the target viewer, trust and
compliance, re-recording mixer), each run twice with the A/B positions swapped. Each reviewer read per-second contact
sheets, full frames on every card, the transcript, a card list and the mix (the mixer measured it; no reviewer listened).
A merge step relabelled the cuts P and Q so it stayed blind; a devil's advocate then attacked the winner.

## Result

| Lens | Order 1 (P in A) | Order 2 (P in B) | Mean P / Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct-response editor | Q | Q | 5.55 / 7.10 |
| target viewer | Q | Q | 5.85 / 7.15 |
| re-recording mixer | Q | Q | 4.50 / 6.75 |
| trust and compliance | P | P | 6.25 / 4.50 |
| retention editor | Q | P | 6.60 / 6.45 |
| cinematic designer | Q | P | 6.05 / 6.50 |

Votes Q 8, P 4; mean 6.41 against 5.80. Two lenses preferred whichever cut sat in slot B, so their four votes say
nothing; the four lenses that answered the same in both orders voted Q 6 to 2. The new engines did not win on their own:
the old structure did.

## Why the re-edit lost (each raised by two reviews or more)

1. Structure: the offer (free to set, the closer, the split) was first heard at 36-41 s, against 11 s in Q.
2. Finish: a measured sky pull-down, a tracked face lift and grain read as "a bright halo around the head and cap and a
   blotchy, posterized sky" at phone size (6 reviews). Q had no finish pass.
3. Overlay cards: flat dark rectangles with body type near 20 px at 1920 wide (about 6-8 px on a phone), and frames where
   the box shows without its text (an empty container on entry and exit).
4. A count-up on a sourced figure flashed intermediate values that are not sourced numbers (all 12 reviews).
5. A fine-print line under a verified claim read as taking the claim back.
6. Sound: a stock "sub" file 65 s long and an "impact" 51 s long droned at 21 Hz from 54 s to the end; the track's own
   fade took the bed from about -27 to -55 LUFS under the ask; integrated loudness 1 LU under the target.

## What the re-edit did better (taken into the next cut)

The bold proof number as a full-screen slam over the dimmed speaker (shown once, with a qualifier), the mock of the real
landing page with a cursor click, captions from the first frame, hand labels tied to the hands, a shorter cut and more
face time (24 % hidden against 35 %).

## Claims that did not survive checking

- "Zipper noise from stepped gain automation on a 100 ms grid above 12 kHz" (mixer): the music stem's high band
  autocorrelates at 99 and 197 ms, and so does the raw track before any processing (0.18 and 0.72 at 30-70 s); at the
  track's 152 BPM a sixteenth is 98.7 ms and an eighth 197.4 ms. Those were hi-hats.
- The merge step's fix list took its timecodes from transcript-line boundaries; the devil's advocate found two that would
  have cut mid-sentence. Cut points come from the word map, never from a reviewer's timecode.

## Process notes for the next panel

Run both orders and count only lenses that agree with themselves; give the merge step neutral labels; have a devil's
advocate check the winner against the house rules the reviewers did not check (type families, loudness on the encoded
file, caption line breaks, speech rate); re-judge the fixed cut instead of asserting the fixes worked.
